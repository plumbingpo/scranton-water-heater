Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Some Scranton, PA homeowners are finishing a basement or building an addition and need a second heater. Others are moving a unit out of a cramped closet and into the garage, upgrading to a larger tank for a growing household, or converting from one fuel to another. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. Scranton Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Every installation starts with sizing and location. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We talk it all through in plain language and then give you a firm price.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. We run and pressure-test new gas piping, add a sediment trap, and route venting exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines go in with accessible shutoffs and proper transitions between dissimilar metals, and the relief valve is piped full size to a safe discharge point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Then we fill the heater, confirm draft and combustion on gas models, and set a safe temperature. Planning a new installation? Call (570) 227-3150 for an assessment in Scranton.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a new water heater means doing several trades' work at once. Gas piping has to be sized for the heater's input and every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested and leak-free. The vent must meet the manufacturer's rules on diameter, length, pitch, and clearances, or carbon monoxide can find its way indoors. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check before we leave. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, we will tell you directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
